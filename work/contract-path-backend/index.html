The feature was the whole contract path, not the payment call

A product backend had to reach launch with accounts, payments, private documents, mail, cache, and backups behind it. I treated the payment as unfinished until the fiscal receipt existed.

01Safe context

The backend supported a product sold under a contract. Money, access to private documents, and the record of what the customer had bought all depended on the same state.

A payment provider returning success is an intermediate event. Until the internal state and the fiscal receipt agree with it, the user flow is not finished.

02Constraints

  • The launch date was fixed, so the work had to reach a working path rather than a complete platform.
  • Private documents could not be reachable by anyone holding a direct link.
  • Payment callbacks arrive more than once and out of order, and that had to be normal rather than an incident.

03What was mine

I implemented the payment handling, the access checks on private files, and the operational paths the launch needed, and I ran the end to end verification myself.

Where the specification stopped, deciding what counted as a finished payment was my call.

04Team and agent boundary

Agents helped with boilerplate and with reading provider documentation. Signature handling, idempotency keys, and the definition of a finished payment were written and checked by me.

For the money path I did not accept generated code until I had traced the failure and retry branches by hand.

05Alternatives I weighed

  1. Treat the provider callback as the source of truth

    Simplest to build. It fails the first time a callback is replayed or arrives twice, and it leaves no internal record to reconcile against. Rejected.

  2. Idempotent handling keyed on the payment, with signature checks first

    More work up front and a harder test setup. It survives replays and gives one place to answer what state a payment is in. This is what I built.

  3. Serve private documents from public storage with unguessable names

    Rejected. An unguessable link is still a link, and it does not expire when access should.

06Decision

Verify the signature before any write, make the state change idempotent on the payment identity, keep private documents behind an access check rather than an obscure URL, and treat the fiscal receipt as the end of the path.

The first version also trusted a price calculated in the browser. I moved the calculation to the server and kept the browser result only as something to reconcile against, because a value supplied by the client is input, not authority.

07Implementation

  • Accounts and the state a purchase moves through.
  • Signature validation and idempotent payment handling.
  • Server-side price calculation with a live comparison against the client path.
  • Private documents served behind an access check.
  • Transactional mail, caching, and an operational backup flow.

Provider names, the queue and storage choices, and the retention window for backups are left out because together they would identify the system.

08How I verified it

I followed one payment from creation through the provider callback and the internal state to the fiscal receipt, then repeated the run with failures injected. Before real money moved, the same path was exercised with a signed callback simulation, including a bad signature and an idempotent replay.

  • Duplicate and out of order callbacks replayed against a live state.
  • Failed and abandoned payments checked for a state that a human can read.
  • Private document access checked while signed out and while signed in as another account.
  • A restore from backup exercised rather than assumed.

The automated suite grew from 235 to 663 tests. I still finished with four independent audit passes. They left the payment core clean and found defects around it, including a locale-dependent decimal comma that had stopped two configurators and made a third calculate zero delivery.

09Result

The backend reached the live server with the contract path working end to end. A real payment travelled from the storefront through the callback and internal state to a fiscal receipt, while a replay resolved to the same internal payment instead of creating another one.

10What this does not prove

This entry describes delivery and my own verification. It is not an external security audit, and it makes no claim about uptime, load, or the commercial performance of the product.

Signature checking and idempotency reduce a known class of failures. They do not make a payment path secure on their own.

11What I would change now

I would keep the callback replay and ordering cases as named regression tests instead of relying on a large suite and an end-to-end simulation. That makes the guarantee visible when the payment code changes.

I would also add a reconciliation job that compares internal state against the provider on a schedule, so a missed callback surfaces without a customer reporting it.
